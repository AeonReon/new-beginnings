# What is actually offered to parents in Northern Ireland

**Researched 2026-09-09.** The question asked: outside government support aimed at
families in difficulty, what exists for parents who want their child to *excel* —
and would pay for it? Plus: what are we missing from the schools map?

Everything below is sourced. Where I could not verify something I say so rather
than filling the gap.

---

## 1. The market splits in two, and nothing sits in the middle

**The free/government half is real and large, and it is aimed at difficulty.**
Sure Start runs **38 projects** across NI for children 0–4, and eligibility is by
ward — it is explicitly for *"families living in the most disadvantaged areas."*
It bundles health visiting, speech and language, parenting courses, fathers'
groups and the funded 2–3 year old Developmental Programme.
(familysupportni.gov.uk, northerntrust.hscni.net)

Alongside it: Home-Start (home visiting), Barnardo's, Family Support NI's
directory, and **Parenting Focus** — the charity formerly called Parenting NI,
supporting parents here since 1979. All good work. All framed around support,
coping, and getting through.

**The paid half is enrichment, and it is sold by the hour or the term.** Youth
Lyric charges **£10/hour** for children's drama in Belfast. Kumon in the UK runs
about **£50–£150 per month, per subject**. Stagecoach, Belfast School of
Performing Arts, Lyric Theatre classes and the music-lesson market sit in the
same band. (youthlyric.co.uk, brighterly.com/blog/kumon-cost)

**Nobody found is doing the thing in between:** a paid, structured, ambitious
offer for a family where nothing is wrong. That is the gap the philosophy names,
and this search did not find anyone in Northern Ireland occupying it.

## 2. The £97/month question — it is the wrong category, in a good way

UK **parenting memberships are priced as cheap content**, and every one found is
support-shaped:

| Product | Price | Framing |
|---|---|---|
| Parenting UK (Family Lives) | from **£2/mo** | membership body, support |
| Level Up Club (children & tech) | **£6.99/mo** | modules + expert Q&A |
| Positivity Hub | subscription | "non-judgemental support", drop-in clinics |
| Your Parenting Mojo | subscription | evidence-based strategies, coaching |

Selling a **£97/month** product into *that* category is impossible — buyers
anchor at seven pounds. Selling it against **what the same family already spends
on their child** is a different conversation entirely:

| What they already pay | Monthly equivalent |
|---|---|
| NI independent day school (£6,540–£15,810/yr) | **£545 – £1,318** |
| Campbell College prep (£7,321/yr) | **£610** |
| Kumon, one subject | **£50 – £150** |
| Weekly drama class at £10/hr | **~£40** |
| Two or three activities a week | **£100 – £200** |

(newsletter.co.uk private school fees; ISC; Campbell College)

**So £97/month is not expensive — it is less than one child's activities**, and
about a sixth of prep-school fees. And £1,000/month is precisely the band NI
families already pay for private day school, which proves the willingness exists
locally. The pricing works. The **category framing** is what has to be right:
this is education and advantage, not parenting advice.

## 3. Alternative education supply in NI is genuinely thin

- **One full alternative school.** Holywood Steiner School (1975, ages 3–17)
  appears to be the only complete alternative-philosophy school in NI.
- **No democratic/Sudbury school** — still true; nearest are across the border.
- **Microschools barely exist here, and are legally awkward in the Republic**,
  where every family in a pod must individually register with Tusla or the group
  risks being treated as an unregistered school. NI's home-education law is more
  permissive, which is a genuine structural advantage worth understanding
  properly. (homeschoolstartguide.com — secondary source, verify before relying)
- **Home-ed groups are small and volunteer-run**: HEdNI, North Down Home
  Education Co-op (monthly, Bangor), Calvary Chapel Belfast co-op. NI has fewer
  in-person groups than mainland UK.
- **Forest school is the one live, growing category.** NIFSA (charity NIC100975)
  trains and accredits leaders and works with hundreds of schools and nurseries.

## 5. Is anyone doing the whole ecosystem?

**Not in Northern Ireland or Ireland, on this search.** The closest analogues are
all American:

- **Wild + Free** — the nearest model by shape: a paid homeschool membership with
  monthly content bundles *and local meetup groups*. Community plus content plus
  philosophy, which is our shape. **Pricing unverified** — their site returns 403
  to automated requests; check it manually, it is the most useful single number
  in this document.
- **Acton Academy** — 300+ learner-driven microschool campuses across ~30
  countries, Socratic, guides forbidden from answering directly. No verified
  UK or Ireland campus found; a search result suggesting "Dublin" refers to
  **Dublin, Ohio.** Do not repeat that as an Ireland presence.
- **Prenda, KaiPod, Wildflower Montessori, Alpha School** — US microschool
  networks. Alpha is already a tradition page in this app.

Nobody found combines **apps + resources + local meet-ups + a school + a
philosophy** for families, anywhere in these islands. That is either a real
opening or a sign it is hard to hold together — probably both. It is at minimum
an honest claim we can make carefully: *we have not found anyone else doing this.*

## 7. What this means

1. **Do not compete in the parenting-support category.** It is well served,
   correctly free, and priced at £7/month. Say so generously and move on.
2. **Compete in the education-and-advantage category**, where NI families already
   spend £500–£1,300 a month without blinking.
3. **Forest school is the closest live thing to us**, and it is sessions, not
   settings — nobody is offering the whole week, the community and the parent's
   own path together.
4. **Northern Ireland's permissive home-education law is a structural advantage**
   over the Republic. Worth understanding properly before anyone builds on it.
5. **The claim to make is the gap, not the rivalry**: *where does an ordinary
   family go to be helped to fly?* This research supports that question being
   genuinely unanswered here.
